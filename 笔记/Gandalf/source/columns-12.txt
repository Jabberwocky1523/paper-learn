To keep each decision cheap, the move door (§3.4) checks, for each
slot size, only the longest-lived DRAM entry as a candidate to move
to the SSD. A move is then blocked whenever that one entry’s score
falls below its write fee, even if another entry of the same size
would be worth writing. To measure what this costs, we let the
move door check up to 64 candidates per slot size, with Kimi-K3,
100GB of DRAM, a 1TB SSD and 𝛼 = 1. Checking more candidates
does not lower Gandalf ’s miss ratio: it stays at 0.823 on TraceA
and 0.036 on AIPerf for every number of candidates. On TraceA,
the extra candidates only move entries that are not read again, so
the SSD writes grow by 1.4% (501 to 508GiB) and the SSD lifetime
shortens from 271 to 268 days; on AIPerf, the first candidate is never
blocked, so the result is identical. A single candidate per slot size
therefore loses no hits.
6 Related Work
Tiered KV-cache systems. CachedAttention, FlashGen, LMCache,
and Mooncake extend GPU memory with host DRAM, SSD, or
distributed storage [7, 13, 16, 36], while TENURE and placement
studies decide where KV caches should reside across these tiers [28,
43]. InfiniGen and Tutti instead reduce lower-tier access overhead
through selective fetching and GPU-directed SSD I/O [20, 37]. These
systems primarily optimize capacity, latency, or throughput rather
than SSD endurance.
KV admission, eviction, and compression. Prior policies predict
reuse from prefix structure, recency, frequency, semantics, queued
demand, or workload history [12, 22, 33, 34, 44, 46, 48, 49]. MeritKV,
Bidaw, LRU-K, and NVIDIA KVBM further incorporate reuse utility,
storage cost, or write awareness [14, 17, 30, 35]. Orthogonal tech-
niques reduce the footprint through token eviction, quantization,
or model-level KV compression [8, 25, 53]. Most of these techniques
do not jointly manage DRAM placement, SSD admission, and SSD
eviction using attention-type reuse heterogeneity and an explicit
endurance cost.

Flash-aware caching and storage. General flash caches control
wear through admission filtering and write-rate constraints [10, 47,
51], while offline work extends Bélady-style analysis to account for
flash erasures [6]. Device- and interface-level techniques reduce
physical writes through retention management, stream placement,
and cache-aware reclamation [24, 26, 27, 39]. For LLM serving,
HiFC, FlashAccel, and HBFlex optimize flash data paths, writeback,
and reclamation [15, 45, 54]. These mechanisms are complementary
to Gandalf because they do not use attention semantics to value
individual KV states.
Lifetime-aware KV placement. Most closely related, Lachesis
places agent KV segments across HBM and high-bandwidth flash
using lifetimes inferred from the agent harness [50]. In contrast,
Gandalf targets host DRAM and SSD, derives reuse differences
from hybrid-attention semantics without harness-specific informa-
tion, and jointly considers capacity, recomputation benefit, and SSD
write cost.
7 Conclusion
This paper presents Gandalf , an SSD-based KV cache offloading
system that uses the attention type associated with each KV cache
object to predict its likelihood of future reuse. Gandalf retains
short-lived linear-attention states in DRAM, while offloading full-
attention KV caches with high reuse potential to SSD. Its placement
policy balances the expected cache hits from each SSD write against
the resulting device wear. By filtering writes based on both reuse
potential and wear cost, Gandalf preserves cache hit rates while
extending SSD lifetime. Across a range of hybrid models and hard-
ware capacities, Gandalf improves SSD lifetime by 2–7.3× over the
best baseline. As KV caches continue to outgrow available memory,
we hope Gandalf motivates future KV cache systems to consider
not only the performance benefits of SSD offloading, but also the
wear caused by each write. We leave the extension of Gandalf to
other types of flash-based storage as a future work.
