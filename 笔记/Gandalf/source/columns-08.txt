Since not all baselines are designed to operate with DRAM and
SSD, for evaluation fairness, we augment each baseline with both
tiers as a capacity expansion to GPU HBM, thereby increasing their
effective cache capacity while preserving their original admission
and victim selection rules. A KV cache entry is admitted to lower
tiers only if it is evicted from the upper tier. Eviction happens as a
side effect of admission, and the eviction policy of each baseline is
retained as is. For all baselines that has hyperparameters, we sweep
the hyperparameter space to show the best configuration on each
trace.
Workload Traces. We evaluate Gandalf on real-world traces
with known timestamps and request sequences, including:
(1) Qwen Bailian Trace A [44], the trace from Aliyun Bailian
inference service that contains 46.4% multi-turn conversa-
tions with text, file understanding, multimodal understand-
ing, and search tasks;
(2) Claude Code WEKA SemiAnalysis trace [40], Claude Code
agentic serving traces showing multi agents, subagents, and
compacted traces;
(3) AIPerf Trace [9], a trace generated using the AIPerf bench-
mark tool that contains 400k requests, where each session
contains 100 turns of user queries and responses.
The statistics of the traces are summarized in Table 3.
Models and Cache Sizes. Throughout the experiments, we
evaluate Gandalf and the baselines on Kimi-K3, a representative
hybrid model having 72% linear attention layers, a typical ratio for
many hybrid LLMs. We set the DRAM cache size to 100GB and vary
the SSD size: 1TB, 4TB, following the common SSD capacity range
for enterprise SSDs. This DRAM size is chosen to hold less than
1% of the peak active KV cache size of most of the traces shown in
Table 3, which is a reasonable assumption for a production system
with limited DRAM capacity.
Metrics. We evaluate Gandalf and the baselines on the follow-
ing metrics:
• SSD lifetime, measured in years, is the time until the SSD
reaches its write endurance limit, given the total bytes writ-
ten (TBW) and the drive writes per day (DWPD) specifica-
tion.

5.2 End-to-end Performance
Figure 7 compares Gandalf with the baselines on TraceA, Claude-
Code and AIPerf with the Kimi-K3 model, 100 GB of DRAM and
a 1 TB SSD. Gandalf , as well as other baselines with trade-off
hyperparameters, are shown as lines through their hyperparameter
space. Gandalf uses the write-fee multiplier 𝛼 (0–64), LRU-K uses
𝐾 (1–10), HotPrefix uses the frequency threshold 𝐹 (1–10), and
Marconi uses its own 𝛼 (0–2).
SSD lifetime. In this hardware configuration, at its lowest miss
ratio (𝛼 = 0), Gandalf extends the SSD lifetime over the lowest-
miss baseline by 2× on TraceA, 3.3× on ClaudeCode, and 6.5× on
AIPerf. It does so by writing 2.0–6.5× fewer bytes to the SSD. Raising
𝛼 trades hits for a longer lifetime. On ClaudeCode, every baseline
point with a miss ratio of up to 0.47 lasts 2.9–10.3× shorter than
Gandalf at an equal or lower miss ratio. Only Marconi with 𝛼 ≥ 1
and HotPrefix with 𝐹 ≥ 7 last longer than Gandalf ’s longest-
lived point, and they miss 4.5–6.1× more. On AIPerf, every baseline
point except Marconi with 𝛼 ≥ 0.5 lasts 1.0–23.8× shorter than
Gandalf at an equal or lower miss ratio. Marconi last 1.3× longer
than Gandalf ’s longest-lived point, but results in 2.2× more cache
misses. On TraceA, Gandalf reaches 57.7 months at a 0.839 miss
ratio, 4.1× the 12.7 months of the longest-lived baseline, HotPrefix
with 𝐹 = 10, at the same miss ratio. LRU-K works in the opposite
direction, where increasing 𝐾 trades a lower miss ratio for a longer
lifetime.
Cache miss ratio. Gandalf reaches the lowest miss ratio of all
policies on TraceA and on AIPerf. On ClaudeCode, Marconi with
𝛼 = 0 and LRU miss slightly less than Gandalf (0.045 and 0.047 vs.
0.052), but write 3.3× more to the SSD.
In summary, with DRAM size of 100GB and 1TB SSD, Gandalf im-
proves SSD lifetime by 7.3× on average while maintaining or even
reducing the cache miss ratio.
