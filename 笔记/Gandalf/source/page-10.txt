5.6 Where does the improvement come from?
Avoid writing non-reused linear states in SSD. Gandalf avoids
writing linear states that will not be reused multiple times, such as
those at every turn of a multi-turn conversation, to the SSD. Fig-
ure 10 shows, over time, the fraction of SSD writes that contribute
to cache hits at least once, for Gandalf and LRU-K on ClaudeCode
with Qwen3.5-27B, 100GB of DRAM and a 1TB SSD. Throughout
the trace, a higher fraction of Gandalf ’s SSD writes are hit than
those of LRU-K at 𝐾 = 1, for both full-attention KV and linear
states.
LRU-K at 𝐾 = 1 writes 120.6k linear states to the SSD, and only
21% of them are ever hit there: 43% are never requested again, and
45% had already served their one hit from DRAM before being
moved to the SSD, i.e., the state of a turn that the next turn already
reused. A larger 𝐾 does not fix this. LRU-K [35] counts references
per object, which captures hotness for objects that persist across
requests, such as a user’s KV cache in generative recommendation.
A linear state, however, is superseded at every turn and is referenced
at most about twice, so it reaches 𝐾 = 2 references only after its one
reuse is over: LRU-K drops a state that leaves DRAM before the next
turn arrives, and writes one that the next turn has already reused.
With 𝐾 = 2, LRU-K writes 1.9× fewer linear states (62.6k), but 90%
of them had already been hit before being written, 58% are never
requested again, and only 13% are hit on the SSD; meanwhile its miss
ratio rises from 0.1 to 0.14. Gandalf instead decides by whether
a state will be reused later, not by how often it was referenced: it
writes 8.8× fewer linear states than LRU-K at 𝐾 = 1, and 57% of
them are hit on the SSD.

As the SSD is full, LRU-K gets more selective at admitting cache
to SSD, hence the cache hit rate decreases. Gandalf , however,
maintains a high cache hit rate throughout.
Avoid writing rarely reused full-attention KV in SSD. We
also increase the reusability of the full-attention KV cache entries
that are written to the SSD, by filtering out those that are unlikely
to be reused in the future, such as those at the end of a conversation
or session. Gandalf writes 33% fewer full-attention KV chunks
than LRU-K at 𝐾 = 1 (21.5M vs. 32.1M), and 61% of them are hit on
the SSD, compared to 50% for LRU-K. Of the chunks LRU-K writes,
9.6% are never requested again, compared to 5.3% for Gandalf , so
Gandalf writes 2.7× fewer such chunks (1.1M vs. 3.1M). Despite
writing fewer chunks, Gandalf serves 18% more full-attention hits
from the SSD (265M vs. 225M), i.e., 12.3 hits per chunk written
compared to 7.0 for LRU-K. Together, Gandalf writes 92TB to the
SSD instead of 153TB, which extends the SSD lifetime by 1.7× (98
vs. 59 days) at a similar miss ratio (0.099 vs. 0.098).
Attention type-aware placement. Gandalf places linear states
in DRAM and full-attention KV in the SSD. Figure 11 shows where
the cache hits of each kind are served from, for Gandalf and LRU-K
at 𝐾 = 1 in the same setting as above. Gandalf serves 87% of its
full-attention KV hits from the SSD and 90% of its linear-state hits
from DRAM. LRU-K, which places objects by recency regardless of
their kind, serves 74% of its full-attention KV hits from the SSD and
only 67% of its linear-state hits from DRAM since its DRAM also
holds full-attention KV that Gandalf would have moved to the
SSD. To serve the remaining 33% (27.0k hits) from the SSD, LRU-K
writes 120.6k linear states to it, 4.5 writes per hit, whereas Gan-
dalf serves 8.0k linear-state hits from the SSD with 13.7k writes,
1.7 writes per hit.
In summary, the improvement of Gandalf comes from leveraging
attention type as a proxy for reusability, which: (1) avoids writing
linear states at the end of each rounds to the SSD, (2) avoids writing
rarely reused full-attention KV to the SSD, and (3) places linear states
in DRAM and full-attention KV in the SSD.
